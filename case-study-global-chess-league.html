Global Chess League S4

Brief

Tech Mahindra needed to elevate the Global Chess League Season 4 — the world's largest franchise chess league — into a watchable, social-first esports spectacle for a modern digital audience.

The brief was simple in ambition and complex in execution: make chess feel as alive, social, and culturally urgent as a BGMI Major. Capture a non-endemic audience that doesn't normally watch chess. And do it across 9 consecutive days of live broadcast with zero room for error.

Challenge

Chess has always been a spectator sport for the deeply invested. The challenge was making it watchable for everyone else — the 500 million Indian gamers who scroll past chess content because it feels slow, static, and hard to follow.

Tech Mahindra had invested in the league as a brand asset, but the broadcast format hadn't evolved. Single-camera setups, no graphics, no commentary that spoke to a younger audience. The product was technically sound but emotionally flat.

We had 9 days. 6 camera angles. A commentary desk. And one mandate: make the Global Chess League S4 feel like the esports event it deserved to be.

Approach

8Bit Creatives was named the Official Broadcast & Production Agency — not a sponsor, not a media buyer, the actual production house. That meant we owned the end-to-end broadcast: camera plan, commentary, graphics, social integration, and distribution.

The approach was built on three pillars:

Talent-first commentary
We cast commentators who could speak both chess and gaming fluently — people who could explain a Sicilian Defense to someone who's never played, while keeping the energy of a BGMI clutch moment.
Real-time telemetry
We built a custom graphics layer that showed real-time chess analytics — move history, position evaluation, time pressure — in a visual language borrowed from esports broadcasts (think minimap overlays, kill feeds, and round timers).
Social-first distribution
The broadcast wasn't just on one platform. We simultaneously distributed across YouTube, Loco, and social media — with clip-ready moments designed for Reels, Shorts, and Twitter/X.

Solution

End-to-end live broadcast production in Bengaluru (Sep 5-13, 2026).

6 camera angles · 9 days · 0 production errors

The studio setup included 6 camera angles covering the main board, player reactions, commentary desk, audience, and B-roll. A custom graphics package rendered real-time position evaluations and move analysis — making every move feel like a play in a sportscast, not just a piece sliding on a board.

The commentary desk was staffed by a rotating team of chess experts and gaming-native voices — ensuring both depth and accessibility. The social team clipped key moments in real-time, pushing them to Reels, Shorts, and Twitter/X within minutes of happening live.
